## 1. Summary and verdict

`scroll-craft` is one Claude Code / Codex skill for one product: a premium, scroll-driven marketing landing page. It is not a general design methodology like CGSpec; it is closer to what CGSpec's `cgspec-ui-ux` skill would look like if it only ever built one kind of screen, written by someone who shipped ten of them and kept notes on what broke.

Verdict for CGSpec: worth studying, nothing to import wholesale. Its prose rules (taste.md, uniqueness.md) are sharper and more specific than anything comparable in `craft.md`, because they are scoped to one page type instead of every screen a product might need. Its mechanical verification (`shoot.mjs`, `worldflight-assert.mjs`) checks things CGSpec's `visual_audit.py` and `journey.py` do not check at all: whether a scrub video actually advances under scroll, and whether a fading line of copy ever reaches full opacity. Its "fingerprint gate" against a hand-written registry is a real idea for cross-build uniqueness that CGSpec has no equivalent of. Against that, it depends on a paid external API for any generated imagery, it has only ever run on Windows, and several of its enforcement points are a script a human must remember to run rather than a hook a commit gate can block on.

## 2. What the skill does end-to-end

1. **Interview (Step 0).** Eight fixed questions asked in one pass: vibe words and references, the scroll journey in the client's words, the energy curve, the feeling curve and the one peak moment, a signature-move seed, a style-family choice, world-vs-scenes structure, and existing assets. Answers (or an explicit creative delegation, or a marked `Self-authored, not interviewed` fallback) are written verbatim to `BRIEF.md` before anything else happens.
2. **Bootstrap.** Run `doctor.mjs`, then `workspace.mjs --ensure` to resolve one shared directory (`SCROLLCRAFT_HOME`, then a `.scrollcraft.json`, then a project-root fallback) holding every build and the single cross-project `FINGERPRINTS.md` registry.
3. **Brief (Step 1).** A handful of questions the interview did not cover, then a four-to-seven-beat customer journey written before any screen exists.
4. **Grammar, gate, score (Step 2).** Pick one of eight named page grammars (filmic one-shot, chaptered editorial, live surface, continuous world/"worldflight", typographic poster, gallery, split stage, rhythmic cutlist); invent one bespoke "signature move"; check the plan against every row in `FINGERPRINTS.md` (must differ on at least 4 of 6 named dimensions from every existing row, not just on average); only then write the feeling curve and a per-beat device score table.
5. **Assets (Step 3).** Either the client's own photos/footage, or images and clips generated through the `kie.ai` API using one reused "style preamble" per build, encoded with a dense-GOP ffmpeg pass (`encode.sh`) specifically so scroll-scrubbing doesn't play like "mud".
6. **Build (Step 4).** Real semantic HTML driven by `data-sc-*` attributes that a shared, never-edited-per-project `engine/scrollcraft.js` + `.css` reads at runtime; the page is themed through six CSS custom properties and two font picks, never by forking the engine.
7. **Verify (Step 5).** `serve.mjs` serves the build; `shoot.mjs` walks it headless at desktop, mobile (390x844) and `prefers-reduced-motion` and writes screenshots plus `report.json` and a `sheet.png` contact sheet; a human then does a "feel check" the harness explicitly says it cannot do, and is told to test on a real phone because headless Chrome cannot reproduce iOS video decode, autoplay policy or touch scrolling.
8. **Output.** A short report (grammar choice and why the other seven lost, fingerprint-gate result, journey, feeling curve, peak, feel-check diff, what was verified and what was not) plus one appended row to `FINGERPRINTS.md`.

Devices (`references/devices.md`, 552 lines) catalogue nine scroll mechanisms (scrub, pin, reveal, pan, parallax, kinetic text, etc.) each with its own markup contract and failure modes. `worldflight.md` (349 lines) is a separate spec for the "continuous world" grammar: one fixed camera stage, scroll-driven legs with weighted spans, no cuts.

## 3. Mechanical enforcement vs. prose and taste

| Mechanism | File | What it actually checks | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| `scripts/doctor.mjs` | preflight | Node >= 18, a full (non-stripped) ffmpeg build by filter count, `playwright-core` resolvable from the build folder specifically, a findable Chrome/Edge binary, `KIE_AI_API_KEY` presence, workspace resolution. Exits non-zero on a required failure. | Mechanical, deterministic, run-before-anything |
| `scripts/workspace.mjs` | workspace resolution | Resolves and creates the one shared builds+registry directory; seeds an empty `FINGERPRINTS.md` from `templates/FINGERPRINTS.md`. | Mechanical |
| `scripts/shoot.mjs` (655 lines) | post-build verification | Launches headless Chromium with pointer-lock/capture disabled; walks each act at six scroll positions; waits for scrub video to actually settle; flags **dead scroll** (positions where nothing on screen changed), **cues that never reach full opacity** (a line of copy whose max observed opacity across the walk stays below 1), **frozen clips** (a scrub video whose decoded frame never advances — "looks exactly like a paused film"), and **contrast measured on the composited page** per text line at that line's brightest underlying frame, graded against WCAG-style 3:1 / 4.5:1 thresholds. Writes `report.json` and an ffmpeg-built `sheet.png` contact sheet (skipped, not failed, without a full ffmpeg). | Mechanical, rendered-DOM level |
| `scripts/worldflight-assert.mjs` (273 lines) | continuous-world mode only | Desktop and reduced-motion passes asserting: segment-weight geometry sums to the declared stage length, the scrub playhead's lerp trace actually moves across distinct values rather than sitting on one, and seam bands between legs. Prints `PASS`/`FAIL` per assertion and exits 1 on any failure. | Mechanical, mode-specific |
| `scripts/kie.mjs` | asset generation | Calls `kie.ai`'s `createTask`/`recordInfo` job API for stills and clips, and a `probe` subcommand that reads account credit. Not a correctness check — a paid generation client. | Mechanical API client, not a gate |
| the fingerprint gate | `FINGERPRINTS.md` + `references/uniqueness.md` | A **prose procedure**, not a script: the agent is told to read the registry table and judge "4 of 6 dimensions differ from every row" itself. No `.mjs` enforces this; nothing fails a build automatically if the agent skips it or miscounts. | Prose, human/agent-graded |
| `references/taste.md` (304 lines), `references/feel.md` (277 lines) | spacing, type, colour, depth, motion rules; the feeling curve and "one engineered peak" rule | Entirely prose, explicitly framed as "a check on the rendered result, not on intention" but with no script that measures spacing rhythm, peak placement, or type-scale adherence. The author tells the agent to build without announcing the checklist and to self-grade against it. | Prose only |
| the hard-rules table in `SKILL.md` (e.g. ban `em dash`, ban `01/06` counters, ban gradient text, ban autoplaying audio) | `SKILL.md` | A list of "ship-blockers," enforced by nothing but the agent reading and obeying the table at build time. `shoot.mjs` does not grep the HTML for any of these strings. | Prose only, zero static-analysis layer |

The gap is consistent and explicit in the source itself: `shoot.mjs`/`worldflight-assert.mjs` check what a headless browser can observe about one rendered page (does it move, does text ever appear, is contrast adequate). Nothing in the repo does source-level static analysis (the equivalent of CGSpec's `visual_audit.py` static pass) — no regex over the HTML for the banned patterns, no check that the fingerprint gate was actually run, no CI hook, no pre-commit gate. Every one of the eight interview questions, the grammar choice, the signature-move requirement, and the entire taste floor are enforced only by an agent reading `SKILL.md` and `references/*.md` faithfully. `references/verify.md` itself says plainly what the harness "cannot tell you": composition quality, whether the motion is smooth, or whether the page means anything.

## 4. Comparison with CGSpec's own pieces

| scroll-craft | CGSpec equivalent | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `scripts/shoot.mjs` rendered walk (dead scroll, cue opacity, frozen clips, composited contrast) | `engine/visual_audit.py` rendered checks (contrast, overflow, focus, touch targets) + `engine/journey.py` (state assertions) | CGSpec's rendered checks cover static layout/contrast/overflow and scripted journeys with named test IDs; neither checks whether a scrub video's decoded frame is advancing, or tracks a fade's peak opacity across a scroll walk. Gap on CGSpec's side for anything scroll-driven. |
| `scripts/doctor.mjs` preflight | `engine/doctor.py` | Different scope: scroll-craft's doctor checks the local toolchain (ffmpeg build quality, Chrome binary, Node version); CGSpec's `doctor.py` checks the project against the CGSpec spec (structure, frozen files, index). Not a true overlap. |
| the fingerprint gate (`FINGERPRINTS.md`, 4-of-6 dimensions) | no direct equivalent | CGSpec has no mechanism that compares a new screen's structure against every prior screen the same agent built, for the same user, to prevent silent convergence on one template. Gap. |
| `references/taste.md` (spacing, type, colour, depth) | `skills/cgspec-ui-ux/references/craft.md` + `skills/cgspec-ui-ux/references/tokens.dtcg.json` | Comparable in intent (a taste floor plus a token sheet), but CGSpec's version is cross-screen and tied to a measured, source-traced token sheet (method section 6, layer 5b); scroll-craft's taste floor is prose-only with named CSS custom properties but no script verifying a build's computed values against them. |
| `references/assets.md` + `references/worlds.md` (style preamble method) | `skills/cgspec-ui-ux/references/image-word-and-motion.md` | Similar idea — align words to real references before generating — but scroll-craft's is scoped to one visual family (photographic scroll worlds) and ties directly into a single paid generator (`kie.mjs`); CGSpec's is generator-agnostic and requires the user's explicit yes before any paid service, which scroll-craft's workflow does not gate on per build. |
| `references/verify.md` "feel check" (scroll cold, one word per act, diff against `BRIEF.md`'s intended curve) | `skills/cgspec-ui-ux/references/critique-rubric.md` (7 criteria, reviewer other than the author, PASS when no score below 3 and mean at least 3.5) | Both insert a human/independent judgment step after mechanical checks pass. CGSpec's rubric is numeric and has a fixed pass threshold; scroll-craft's feel check is qualitative and diff-based against the brief, with no numeric threshold. |
| no equivalent | inspiration library (`skills/cgspec-ui-ux/references/inspiration-library.md`) | scroll-craft has no persistent, cross-project reference library; each build's references live only in that build's `BRIEF.md`. |
| no equivalent | CGSpec's commit gate / registered-check manifest (`cgspec_hook.py check register`) | scroll-craft's hard-rules table and taste floor are never registered as a check a commit can fail on; they are run only if the agent remembers to run `shoot.mjs` and read the result. Gap on scroll-craft's side. |

## 5. Adopt / Reject / Open — recommendations, not yet approved

**Adopt (proposed):**
- **Dead-scroll and cue-peak-opacity detection for scroll-driven screens.** Target: `engine/visual_audit.py` or a new rendered check in `engine/journey.py`, gated behind the project having any `data-sc-*`-style or scroll-linked animation at all, so static screens pay nothing. Reason: this is a real gap — CGSpec's rendered checks have no notion of "does this position differ from the last one" or "did this fade ever finish," and any future scroll-storytelling screen would ship unverified without it.
- **A fingerprint-style uniqueness register**, scoped per *user* rather than per project, listing structural dimensions of screens already built (not just visual tokens) so a new screen's structure is checked against prior ones before it is proposed. Target: a new file alongside `skills/cgspec-ui-ux/references/inspiration-library.md`, or an extension of it. Reason: CGSpec's critique rubric scores one screen in isolation; nothing currently stops an agent from quietly reusing the same screen shape across unrelated projects for the same user.
- **The "what the harness cannot tell you" disclosure pattern** from `references/verify.md` — explicitly naming, in the tool's own output, what a green run does not cover (here: a real phone's video decoder and autoplay policy). Target: `engine/visual_audit.py`'s PARTIAL/BLOCKED reporting, which already does this in spirit (method section 6, layer 6 conformance rules) but could borrow scroll-craft's more concrete phrasing.

**Reject (proposed):**
- **Importing the device/grammar vocabulary (scrub, pin, reveal, worldflight, the eight grammars) as a CGSpec concept.** It is excellent but scoped to one page archetype (cinematic scroll landing pages); CGSpec is domain-general, and absorbing it would bias `craft.md` toward marketing sites. Keep it as a reference pointer only if a project specifically needs this page type.
- **Running the engine (`scrollcraft.js`/`.css`) itself inside a CGSpec project.** It is a third-party, unversioned-by-CGSpec runtime with its own update cadence and a stated "only ever run on Windows" limitation for its toolchain scripts (not the engine itself, which is portable CSS/JS, but the build pipeline around it is Windows-first).

**Open (needs the user's decision, not a researcher's call):**
- **`kie.ai` is a paid third-party generation API** (`.env.example` names `KIE_AI_API_KEY`; `scripts/kie.mjs` calls `createTask`/`recordInfo`/a credit-`probe` endpoint at `api.kie.ai`). The source states a still "costs cents" and a 5-second clip "costs more," without a specific price list in any file read. CGSpec's own rule (the Rules section of `skills/cgspec-ui-ux/SKILL.md`) already requires the user's explicit yes before a paid service or new tool; this spike did not evaluate `kie.ai`'s pricing, licence, or data-handling terms beyond what the source states, and that evaluation is still open if a future project wants scroll-craft-style generated assets. No recommendation to adopt or reject the service itself is made here — this is a flag, not a verdict.

## 6. Limits of this research

- The skill's scripts (`doctor.mjs`, `workspace.mjs`, `shoot.mjs`, `worldflight-assert.mjs`, `kie.mjs`, `encode.sh`, `serve.mjs`) were read as text only. None were executed, no server was started, and no headless browser was launched, per the task's constraint.
- The two `references/*.html` files (`device-diag.html`, `template.html`) were not rendered or executed, only referenced by name from the markdown that describes them; their exact markup was not independently verified against the prose description.
- `CHANGELOG.md` (597 lines) was not read in full; only `SOURCE-README.md`, `START-HERE.txt`, `SKILL.md`, `taste.md` and `uniqueness.md` heads, and `FINGERPRINTS.md` were read closely enough to quote. Claims about build history beyond the four-then-ten-site narrative in `SOURCE-README.md` and `uniqueness.md` §1 were not cross-checked further.
- No pricing table for `kie.ai` was found in the source; the "cents" / "costs more" language in `SOURCE-README.md` is the only cost information available here, and no external lookup of `kie.ai`'s current pricing was performed.
- This note does not assess whether scroll-craft's claims about its own ten approved sites are accurate, since the client assets and private brief data behind them are explicitly not bundled in the source.
- `QUEUE-001` (AIS-OS, by the same author) is a separate, not-yet-run spike and is not addressed here; see [`RESEARCH-QUEUE.md`](RESEARCH-QUEUE.md).
